\paragraph{Paired differences and their scope.}
On these 2000 official WildJailbreak adversarial harmful prompts, five learned vectors minus four norm-matched random directions give -22.92 pp under mapped HarmBench and -18.68 pp under WildGuard, at fixed $\alpha=1.0$ in Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Both judges and both mapping-confidence strata have negative differences under the frozen cross-judge rule. Intervals condition on these nine vectors. The exact 126-allocation statistic describes unit separation under an exchangeability assumption; its minimum attainable probability does not measure effect size or make the five seeds and four generated directions independent population samples.

Mapped HarmBench absolute rates are descriptive: changing top-1 to top-2 behavior references flips 36.65\% of baseline decisions, and a random mismatched reference yields 1.0\% harmful labels on its 200-row control. WildGuard was trained on data sharing the WildJailbreak source, making this an in-distribution evaluation for that judge. The official prompts were filtered against weak models. Neither judge is human-calibrated; their baseline harmfulness agreement has $\kappa\approx0.51$. The steering strength was not selected on this set, but fit12 training attacks derive from JBB behaviors that may overlap source behaviors here. The exposure layers use an unvalidated behavior mapping.
