\section{Results}
\subsection{Random references and learned residuals}
On val70, the HF baseline ASR is 90.0\%. Three Gaussian directions with norm 5.21 give 68.6\%, 71.4\%, and 80.0\%; eight norm-matched initialization-only vectors span 70.0--85.7\%. The combined eleven-direction mean is 75.7\%, with standard deviation 4.6 percentage points (pp). Thus the perturbation reference alone lowers judge ASR by 4--21 pp. Eight learned vectors span 18.6--30.0\%, providing an increment beyond the sampled random reference, but the judge decrease itself does not establish refusal or removal of harmful content.

On WJB, five learned-seed ASRs lie between 19.3\% and 28.0\%, below all four random-direction ASRs of 35.3--40.3\%. The exploratory unit-level exact rank test has $U=0$ over 126 allocations, with two-sided $p=0.01587$. Only four random directions were sampled. The earlier criterion requiring at least four of five seeds to beat a two-direction reference by 10 pp remains unsuccessful at 3/5; adding directions does not revise it.

The five-seed mean wrapper gap on the separate 292-pair sample is 6.71 pp, with a seed-level interval of $[3.38,10.04]$ pp. The val296-source analysis has a mean of 5.05 pp. This is a same-scale replication on different prompts using the same vectors. Seed rankings correlate with both the earlier gap and legal over-refusal ($\rho\approx0.975$, five points with ties), so the evidence does not isolate a seed-specific style sensitivity from general refusal propensity. The 3-pp threshold followed observations on val296 and does not give special meaning to a seed just exceeding it.

Full-text review of 20 vanilla rule additions identifies nine condition-item outputs whose baseline was already a nonanswer, two substantive fictional-email responses falsely flagged by the rule, and nine content-response-to-refusal changes. Removing the four implicated pair IDs across all seeds changes the mean from 6.71 to 7.22 pp on 288 pairs; this is post-hoc sensitivity, not a revision of the frozen analysis.

The ongoing augmentation experiment uses same-pipeline retrained controls and matched val70 ASR. The first matching results are in Table~\ref{tab:matching}; no holdout augmentation conclusion is reported while generation remains incomplete. Adding wrapped benign examples also changes benign-data volume and halves each old keep weight. A separate unwrapped-data arm on fixed seeds 3--5 is therefore specified. Without that arm, the intervention can only be described as augmentation with 120 benign inputs that include wrappers, not a pure style-matching effect.

\subsection{Zero-training prompts and output filtering}

On Llama, system prompts A and B give 35.7\% and 18.6\% judge ASR. Operational legal additions are 4/388 and 47/388; full model review puts B at 46--49 on the fixed eligible set, but A lacks the same review and baseline tail samples include truncated uncertain outputs. We do not establish a calibrated ordering or equivalence with learned steering.

On Qwen, baseline ASR is 57.1\%; the four A/B conditions span 37.1--40.0\%. Legal-side results are model-annotated ranges without human calibration. Rule and full-review differences are condition-dependent, and an exploratory A-versus-B comparison does not survive correction for six comparisons. Cross-model model-label counts can be juxtaposed descriptively, but do not support absolute over-refusal rankings.

The output Guard receives only the full response text. At threshold 0.5, four baseline Qwen harmful outputs are exactly on the boundary: blocking with $\geq$ leaves 18/70 harmful outputs, versus 22/70 with $>$. At 0.2, baseline plus Guard leaves 9/70 while A\_replace plus Guard leaves 14/70. Among 24 prompts harmful under both conditions, Guard detects 18 baseline responses and 11 A responses, with discordance 7 versus 0 (exploratory exact $p=0.015625$). This demonstrates output-dependent detection, not a confirmed role-play or disclaimer mechanism. Lower thresholds sharply increase benign blocking; no cross-model numerical over-refusal comparison is made.
