\subsection{Random controls change the interpretation of lower ASR}

On the historical success-selected HarmBench and JBB-Behaviors prompt set (val70), the HF baseline ASR is 90.0\%. Three Gaussian directions with norm 5.21 give 68.6\%, 71.4\%, and 80.0\%; eight norm-matched initialization-only vectors span 70.0--85.7\%. The combined eleven-direction mean is 75.7\%, with standard deviation 4.6 percentage points (pp). Thus the perturbation reference alone lowers judge ASR by 4--21 pp. Eight learned vectors span 18.6--30.0\%, providing an increment beyond the sampled random reference, but the judge decrease itself does not establish refusal or removal of harmful content.


On the 300 attacks sampled from the WildJailbreak training split, sampled without filtering for baseline success, the HF baseline ASR is 39.3\%. Four norm-matched random directions change it by $-4.0$ to $+1.0$ pp, while five learned vectors lower it by 11.3--20.0 pp. Learned ASRs are 19.3--28.0\%, below every random ASR (35.3--40.3\%). The exploratory exact rank test gives $U=0$ over 126 allocations and two-sided $p=0.01587$, treating the five seeds and four directions as exchangeable units under the null on shared prompts. The frozen two-direction criterion required at least 4/5 seeds to beat the random mean by 10 pp; only 3/5 did. Using all four directions gives 5/5 as a supplementary comparison, with no change to the frozen decision.

The smaller random-direction changes on WildJailbreak are consistent with an effect of conditioning val70 on prior success, including regression toward the mean, but do not identify that explanation. A separate HF sweep with a mean-difference direction gives nonmonotone ASRs of 84.3, 85.7, 81.4, 72.9, 75.7, and 78.6\% at $\alpha=0.25$ through $1.5$, versus 90.0\% at zero. It is not a random-direction dose experiment.


On the official WildJailbreak evaluation, the learned-minus-random comparison is retained under both judges. It is a conditional comparison of the sampled vectors, not a claim of a universally superior defense.

\subsection{The additional decline accompanies more refusals}
We distinguish harmfulness-judge flips from refusal labels. The completed post-hoc decomposition associates most of the additional score decline with increased refusal, while also finding changes among outputs not labelled as refusals. The unit-level harmfulness and refusal rates are shown together in Table~\ref{tab:wjb-unit-rates}. This association does not establish a causal mechanism, and rule recall can vary with response style.

Among WildGuard nonrefusals, learned-vector harmfulness is lower than for the sampled random directions, but this post-hoc conditional decomposition does not identify a mechanism. It uses direct harmfulness/nonrefusal intersections and separately records invalid labels.

The five-seed mean wrapper gap on all 300 WildJailbreak benign pairs is 6.87 pp. The earlier estimate in the flagged 292-pair baseline-negative stratum is 6.71 pp, with a seed-level interval of $[3.38,10.04]$ pp. The val296-source analysis has a mean of 5.05 pp. This is a same-scale replication on different prompts using the same vectors. Seed rankings correlate with both the earlier gap and legal over-refusal ($\rho\approx0.975$, five points with ties), so the evidence does not isolate a seed-specific style sensitivity from general refusal propensity. The 3-pp threshold followed observations on val296 and does not give special meaning to a seed just exceeding it.

Full-text review of 20 vanilla rule additions identifies nine condition-item outputs whose baseline was already a nonanswer, two substantive fictional-email responses falsely flagged by the rule, and nine content-response-to-refusal changes. Removing the four implicated pair IDs across all seeds changes the mean from 6.71 to 7.22 pp on 288 pairs; this is post-hoc sensitivity, not a revision of the frozen analysis.


\subsection{Positioning zero-training baselines}

On the historical HarmBench and JBB-Behaviors prompt set, Llama system prompts A and B give 35.7\% and 18.6\% judge ASR. Rule-defined new refusals on the full 400-row composite legal set are 4/400 and 47/400. On the marked baseline-negative stratum the corresponding counts are 4/388 and 47/388. Model review puts B at 46--49 in that stratum, but A lacks the same review and baseline tail samples include truncated uncertain outputs. We do not establish a calibrated ordering or equivalence with learned steering.

On Qwen, baseline ASR is 57.1\%; the four A/B conditions span 37.1--40.0\%. Legal-side results are model-annotated ranges without human calibration. Rule and full-review differences are condition-dependent, and an exploratory A-versus-B comparison does not survive correction for six comparisons. Cross-model model-label counts can be juxtaposed descriptively, but do not support absolute over-refusal rankings.

The output Guard receives only the full response text. At threshold 0.5, four baseline Qwen harmful outputs are exactly on the boundary: blocking with $\geq$ leaves 18/70 harmful outputs, versus 22/70 with $>$. At 0.2, baseline plus Guard leaves 9/70 while A\_replace plus Guard leaves 14/70. Among 24 prompts harmful under both conditions, Guard detects 18 baseline responses and 11 A responses, with discordance 7 versus 0 (exploratory exact $p=0.015625$). This demonstrates output-dependent detection, not a confirmed role-play or disclaimer mechanism. Lower thresholds sharply increase benign blocking; no cross-model numerical over-refusal comparison is made.
