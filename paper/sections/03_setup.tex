\subsection{Models and intervention}
 The primary target is Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. A supplementary zero-training evaluation uses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; no Qwen steering vector or alpha sweep is reported. The old trainer learns a 4096-dimensional vector with norm 5.21, 40 Adam steps at learning rate 0.005, 12 fixed attack inputs, and 60 benign keep inputs. Model weights remain frozen. At evaluation, a vector scaled by $\alpha$ is injected once into the last prompt position of layer index 9. Generation is greedy. Harmful evaluation permits 2400 new tokens; the original benign protocol permits 120, with a controlled 400-token follow-up for nine uncertain examples.


\subsection{Prompt samples and historical use}
 All attack sets in this study consist of text behaviors. The selected attack set, val70, contains 14 harmful JBB-Behaviors rows and 56 HarmBench behaviors, with successful PAIR prompts selected against the unmodified Llama model. Selection matters: this is not an unbiased sample of all harmful requests. The historical WildJailbreak attack evaluation contains 300 adversarial harmful prompts sampled from the training split. WildJailbreak also provides a distinct official evaluation split. Two benign samples pair unwrapped originals with adversarially packaged benign requests. One analysis retains 293 pairs from the val296 source after its baseline exclusion; a separate 300-pair sample retains 292 when either-side baseline rule refusals are removed. The latter exclusion was documented after some generations and is not described as an original pre-generation rule.

The planned transfer evaluation retains all 400 HarmBench text rows and 100 harmful JBB-Behaviors rows (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_scope}). The search uses a rule-scored PAIR-style attack on undefended Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Non-refusal and output length guide search. The ten-row pilot preceded the documented protocol freeze, and its alpha-zero results had already been read. Its rows remain part of the 500-row manifest. The search configuration and selection rule were retained without post-pilot tuning; the remaining rows follow the frozen order. System prompts and fixed vectors receive the same frozen per-row prompts. Historical val70 results remain a separate success-selected stratum.

\subsection{Statistics and versioned decisions}
 Five-seed intervals use a $t$ reference with four degrees of freedom. A joint frontier sensitivity also resamples attack and benign items and propagates old-curve uncertainty; unsupported interpolation draws are identified. The WJB nine-unit rank permutation test is exploratory and assumes exchangeability of five learned seeds and four random directions, conditional on shared prompts. Versioned logs preserve amendments and unsuccessful criteria rather than relabeling later supplements as original confirmation.

\paragraph{Cross-model port protocol (pending).} Additional model ports use an attack-refusal operating point on the transferred val70 selection set, subject to a per-seed budget of at most5pp new refusals on the fixed388 legal prompts. Their alpha is not inherited from Llama alpha1.0, so cross-model effect magnitudes are not strength-matched. These ports do not yet contribute evaluated models or results.

\paragraph{Unified fixed targets for pending ports.} Before any new-model vector training, a versioned amendment replaced the conditional native-donor branch with the same twelve fixed Llama donor-generated target texts for Qwen, Gemma and Vicuna. Each model retains its own benign keep continuations. The failed Qwen native-target gate is preserved as a superseded diagnostic, not lowered or erased. The fixed texts are an old-recipe Llama reconstruction; their byte identity to every historical Llama training run is not established. These are uniform text-transfer ports, not certified replicas of the historical native-target runs. Fresh model-specific refusal audits still precede training. Gemma system baselines use a user-turn prefix because its native template rejects a system role.
