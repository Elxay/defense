\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\subsection{Intervention and controls}
We audit a single additive intervention, rather than propose a new defense. Model weights are frozen. A vector is added once during prefill at the last prompt position of a designated transformer block; it is not added at every decoding step. We index the output of zero-based \texttt{layers[j]}, rather than \texttt{hidden\_states[j]}, to avoid an off-by-one change of intervention site. The first round uses Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. Gemma-2-9B-it and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 remain preparation targets until their execution gates and evaluations are complete.

Each model has three learned vectors, trained with seeds 3, 4, and 5, and two prespecified random directions. All six conditions, including the unmodified model, receive the same prompts. Training uses twelve fixed attack inputs and sixty benign keep inputs, forty optimization steps, learning rate 0.005, and the frozen attack-term weight 4.5. The attack targets are the same twelve cached Llama-style refusal texts for every model; benign keep continuations come from the corresponding model. This controls target text across models but makes the refusal target a text-transfer target for Qwen and subsequent ports. A failed native-target admission rule was superseded before new-model training; its diagnostic outputs remain separate. Reconstruction checks do not certify byte identity with every historical Llama training target.

The vector radius is $\rho=0.9M$, where $M$ is the median final-prompt-position residual norm on keep60, with the recorded model-specific rounding. Every runtime vector, learned or random, is scaled to the same selected $\alpha\rho$. We record the vector hash, actual runtime norm, device UUID, hook position, and injection count. Tables report the perturbation relative to $M$, because a shared calibration rule need not select the same relative intervention strength across models.

\subsection{Calibration, separate from testing}
The calibration pool contains 300 adversarial harmful requests, 200 adversarial benign requests, and 200 unwrapped benign requests from the WildJailbreak training split. Its row order, grouping, and overlap audit are frozen before new calibration outputs. Earlier val70 and legal388 selection results are historical diagnostics, not this round's calibration set.

Let $R_0$ be the baseline WildGuard response-refusal rate on the 300 harmful calibration prompts. The target is $R_0+0.5(1-R_0)$. The grid is $\{0.10,0.25,0.50,0.75,1.0,1.25,1.5\}$. A point is feasible only if every seed has at most twenty new refusals among the four hundred benign prompts, counting baseline nonrefusal to condition refusal and retaining worst-case missing-label bounds. Among feasible points we select the one whose three-seed mean attack-refusal rate is closest to the target; ties favor the smaller value. All three seeds share that value. We assume no monotonicity in $\alpha$.

An unreachable target does not permit a wider grid, extra optimization, or dropping a seed. The nearest feasible point remains the operating point, and its results are marked ``target unreachable.'' No feasible nonzero point is a separate outcome. WildGuard refusal is the selection measure and v2 is reported in parallel. Selection generation is capped at 120 new tokens, so this operating point is conditional on a short-reply protocol rather than a claim about an unconstrained refusal propensity.

\subsection{Test data and attack families}
The primary harmful-instruction pool is SORRY-Bench 202503: 440 instructions in 44 categories. Its foundation requests form the direct-request family. We retain the released five persuasion styles, four encodings, and five languages. DeepInception, refusal suppression, and assistant prefill are additional fixed constructions. Prefill has two deployments: P1 adds the vector at the final prefill token, whereas P2 preserves the native assistant-boundary intervention position. Their alpha-zero prompts and replies share one statistical baseline. Rendering and position gates are model-specific; a model without a native system role is not forced into another model's interface.

Search families are PAIR, TAP, and AutoDAN-Turbo. The first two retain their frozen HarmBench search configurations while replacing role models with local models; these are not claimed as original-role reproductions. Search targets the unmodified model, and final prompts are replayed under all six conditions. This measures transfer to defended conditions, not adaptive robustness. AutoDAN-Turbo uses a shared library built from calibration-only warmup requests with the frozen local embedding model. Testing cannot update that library. Its build cost and incomplete requests remain in the resource ledger. Timing pilots are retained in the nested instruction sets, but their ten-row diagnostic summaries are excluded from substantive result tables.

Each family first receives an alpha-zero SORRY-judge gate. A fulfillment rate strictly below 25\% marks no dynamic range and skips the nonzero conditions. Missing labels stay in the denominator; a bound that crosses the gate is reported as unresolved and continues. Search expansion follows the prespecified nested 88, 220, and 440 instruction sets, with two, five, and ten rows per category. Expansion costs exclude future work on failed-gate cells, while all sunk search costs remain in the budget. No gate uses a defended-condition effect.

The cost sets are XSTest safe (250), OR-Bench-Hard (1,319), and WildJailbreak evaluation benign (210); XSTest unsafe (200) is a separate refusal-capability control. Training-overlap rows are retained and flagged, with unseen strata reported alongside the full sets. Utility uses fixed 200-item GSM8K and ARC-Challenge subsets. The completed historical WildJailbreak and PAIR-500 evaluations used a different operating point and sampling history; they appear only as legacy evidence in the appendix.

\subsection{Generation, preprocessing, and judges}
Harmful generation and search-prompt replay use native chat rendering, greedy decoding, and a 2400-new-token cap. Public refusal tests use 120 tokens. Batch size, input order, and length sorting are fixed across conditions within a model--family cell. The accepted English paths are Llama B8 and Qwen B4. Long-family paths require their own headroom and execution checks; a path change applies to the whole family, including alpha zero. A fixed engineering screen permits at most two refusal-label changes per hundred prompts per checked condition relative to single-row generation. Passing this screen does not establish numerical or statistical equivalence. Raw outputs are immutable and cache keys bind their hashes.

SORRY-Bench fulfillment and HarmBench's original-English-instruction classifier are two model-based task-completion measures. WildGuard's primary companion measure is \emph{nonrefusal}, one minus its response-refusal field; its response-harmfulness field is secondary. Nonrefusal is not synonymous with fulfillment. External reference validation uses the released SORRY-Bench human-judgment test split, with split membership, shared instructions, response styles, and class-specific errors reported. It does not calibrate our defended-model outputs.

A style-covered dimension qualifies for wording decisions only under the frozen external $\kappa\geq0.60$ rule with both human classes represented. At least two dimensions must qualify, and every qualified dimension's learned-minus-random identification bound must lie below zero to upgrade wording. All dimensions are reported regardless of qualification. For own English constructions and search families without matching human reference styles, the original all-three-negative rule is retained and the unvalidated response distribution is explicit. Language and encoding results remain descriptive and SORRY-only. No language upgrade follows merely from translating a reply.

Language detection has a fixed seed. English replies pass through; other replies use the frozen local English-translation model. We retain originals, translation failures, and both all-row and originally-English strata. The latter is condition-selected because steering can change output language. Programmatic decoders preserve plaintext passthrough and prespecified ambiguous branches. HarmBench long inputs use the frozen response-token window path with overlap and the original behavior preserved, rather than silent clipping. Each backend passes its own twenty-row execution gate.

\subsection{Contrasts, missingness, and staged reading}
For each model--family cell, the primary contrast is the mean outcome of three learned seeds minus the mean of two random directions, in percentage points. We also report the change relative to alpha zero and the baseline-relative reduction, without treating low-baseline percentages as comparable effect sizes. Intervals cluster by base instruction, retaining all of its transformations; they are conditional on these vectors. Three versus two directions allow only ten label allocations, so a minimum one-sided permutation probability of 0.1 cannot support a claim of permutation significance.

Missing fields receive explicit IDs and full-denominator identification bounds. Refusal association is a post-hoc decomposition of SORRY or HarmBench fulfillment declines with WildGuard or v2 refusal markers; we never explain the nonrefusal proxy with its own complement. Public refusal tables show both learned-minus-baseline and learned-minus-random contrasts, using WildGuard as the primary field and v2 as a parallel, often lower reference rather than a guaranteed lower bound.

Reading is staged: English static families; Chinese and French; encodings and Indian languages; search; and other defenses. Every stage retains the frozen coverage, baseline, contrast, qualification, decomposition, and cost checks, with a timestamp and output hash before the next step. Later stages cannot be redesigned in response to earlier effects. Cache-only scoring may precede reading. An English result is not admitted simply because generation is complete: all required judge coverage and source hashes must first agree.
